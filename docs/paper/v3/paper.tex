\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{OpenCaseLaw}, an open, continuously-refreshed corpus of \textbf{971{,}437} Swiss court decisions across \textbf{108 courts} and \textbf{28 cantons plus federal}, in German, French, and Italian, paired with a citation graph of \textbf{8.65M} distinct outgoing references (\textbf{8.03M} resolved, 92.9\,\%) and a statute graph of \textbf{11.26M} edges over \textbf{283{,}119} distinct provisions. The corpus is bridged to \textbf{5{,}516} federal and \textbf{15{,}722} cantonal laws (\textbf{753{,}842} articles in total), \textbf{362} CC-BY scholarly commentaries, and \textbf{5{,}292} Federal Council Botschaften (Materialien, DE/FR/IT-parallel, with \textbf{381{,}711} full-text paragraphs and \textbf{8{,}124} article-anchored links). The infrastructure is served through a deployed MCP/REST endpoint at \texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch} (31 tools), a Word-add-in client, and a public dashboard. We use the resource to introduce three contributions:
(i)~a first cross-lingual leading-case retrieval benchmark for Swiss law, with author-extracted parallel queries in DE/FR/IT against 50 most-cited decisions, where we measure directional asymmetries (overall MRR@10$=$0.630; IT$\to$DE$=$0.776; DE$\to$FR$=$0.333). We disclose a coupling between query construction and target regestes that limits the realism of the benchmark and discuss it in Section~\ref{sec:limit};
(ii)~a five-rail closing audit pipeline for legal-RAG output, deployed in production, that decomposes hallucination by named error class (case-citation existence, statute resolution, verbatim-quote, decision-date sanity, and an opt-in proposition-grounding judge); and
(iii)~a four-layer dataset health framework with 61 codified checks, drift detection, and a publish-gate that blocks production updates on critical regression. The corpus, citation graph, audit pipeline, and benchmark are released under permissive licences (CC-BY-4.0 / MIT / CC0) with a public dashboard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Legal artificial intelligence has converged on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) as the standard architecture for question answering over jurisprudence. Yet recent measurement studies report fabrication rates of 58--82\,\% on general-purpose LLMs and 17--33\,\% on commercial legal-RAG products~\cite{dahl2024,magesh2024}. The mitigation literature is methodologically sophisticated but concentrated on \emph{English-language, single-jurisdiction} settings where retrieval substrates and ground-truth citation graphs are limited.

Switzerland is the most multilingual major civil-law jurisdiction. The authoritative version of a leading Swiss federal decision may be in German, French, or Italian, and citations cross language boundaries: in our resolved citation graph, \textbf{34.0\,\% of resolved citations are cross-lingual} (Table~\ref{tab:cross_lang_citation}). Building a credible RAG system for Swiss law therefore requires (a)~a corpus that spans the federal--cantonal landscape across all three languages, (b)~a citation and statute graph that can verify generated claims, and (c)~evaluation that measures cross-lingual retrieval as a first-class concern.

We present \textbf{OpenCaseLaw}, an open infrastructure that fills these gaps. We make three contributions:

\paragraph{C1 --- Resource.} We release the largest open, multilingual, federation-complete corpus of Swiss court decisions that we are aware of, with same-day refresh of federal Supreme Court decisions (poller-driven, $\sim$15\,min latency) and nightly refresh of all other federations, a knowledge graph spanning citations, statute references, and Federal Council Botschaften (\emph{Materialien}, the primary source for teleological interpretation in Swiss civil law), and a deployed MCP/REST infrastructure with 31 retrieval and analysis tools.

\paragraph{C2 --- Benchmark.} We introduce a \emph{cross-lingual leading-case retrieval} task. Our headline result (Section~\ref{sec:crosslang}) shows that the BM25+RRF baseline scores MRR@10$=$0.630 averaged across 150 queries (50 leading cases$\times$3 languages), with directional asymmetry: IT$\to$DE retrieves at MRR$=$0.776, exceeding DE$\to$DE on the same targets (0.617), while DE$\to$FR is the hardest cell at 0.333. We disclose that queries are extracted from each language's official regeste, which makes the absolute numbers an upper bound on realistic legal-research queries (Section~\ref{sec:limit}).

\paragraph{C3 --- Verification.} We deploy and evaluate a five-rail closing audit pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:audit}) that decomposes legal-RAG hallucination into named error classes, plus a four-layer dataset-health framework (Section~\ref{sec:qc}) that codifies 61 dataset invariants with drift detection. The framework blocks the production publish on a critical regression.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Open legal corpora.} The Caselaw Access Project~\cite{cap2018} releases 6.7M US decisions but does not publish a citation graph or maintain a retrieval system. The \emph{Open Legal Data} platform~\cite{ostendorff2020} (laws and decisions, OLD36k initial release; OLD201k extension by Milz et al.~2025 grows to 201\,k decisions) and \emph{LeCNet}~\cite{lecnet2025} provide German legal citation networks but no statute graph and no multilingual coverage. \emph{Pile of Law}~\cite{pile2022} and \emph{MultiLegalPile}~\cite{niklaus2023} are pretraining corpora rather than retrieval substrates. Within Swiss legal NLP, \emph{Swiss-Judgment-Prediction}~\cite{niklaus2021} and \emph{SCALE}~\cite{rasiah2023} address narrow tasks (one court, multi-task NLP); we instead release the underlying resource and target retrieval and verification.

\paragraph{Legal-RAG benchmarks.} \emph{LegalBench-RAG}~\cite{pipitone2024} introduces retrieval evaluation for US legal RAG. \emph{Butler\,\&\,Butler 2026}~\cite{butler2026} adds end-to-end correctness$\times$groundedness$\times$retrieval-accuracy decomposition on Victorian criminal law. \emph{LegalRAG}~\cite{legalrag2025} proposes a multilingual hybrid pipeline. \emph{HousingQA / BarExamQA}~\cite{zheng2025} are US-focused multiple-choice. None of these benchmarks measure cross-lingual retrieval where the target authoritative version may be in a different language than the query.

\paragraph{Hallucination measurement and mitigation.} \emph{Dahl et al.\ 2024}~\cite{dahl2024} and \emph{Magesh et al.\ 2024}~\cite{magesh2024} establish hallucination rates on general LLMs and commercial legal-RAG. Self-RAG~\cite{asai2023} and CRAG~\cite{yan2024} propose verification-by-judge and corrective retrieval, both as black-box LLM passes. We instead decompose verification into per-error-class deterministic rails plus an opt-in judge.

\paragraph{Positioning.} OpenCaseLaw is, to our knowledge, the first project that combines (a)~a multilingual federation-complete legal corpus, (b)~a citation and statute graph at this scale, (c)~a cross-lingual leading-case benchmark, and (d)~a deployed verification pipeline, in a single open release.

\section{Resource: corpus}\label{sec:corpus}

\input{tables/corpus_overview.tex}

The OpenCaseLaw corpus comprises \textbf{971{,}437 decisions} from \textbf{108 distinct courts} across \textbf{28 cantons plus federal}. The federal Supreme Court (BGer) is refreshed within approximately 15 minutes of its document-service publication via a dedicated poller; all other federations (BVGer, BStGer, BPatGer, regulatory authorities, 53 cantonal sources, ECHR-Swiss-subset, historical archives) are refreshed by a nightly publish at 03:00 UTC. Coverage spans \textbf{1875--2026} and three official languages: German (449{,}575 / 46.3\,\%), French (441{,}158 / 45.4\,\%), Italian (80{,}704 / 8.3\,\%). The federation aggregates \textbf{54 individual scrapers} ranging from federal courts (BGer, BVGer, BStGer, BPatGer) to per-canton portals to the European Court of Human Rights (Swiss subset; see below), historical archives (BGE 1875--1953, MKG 1915--2025), and regulatory authorities (FINMA, WEKO, ED\"OB, UBI, ELCom, PostCom, ComCom).

\paragraph{ECHR Swiss subset.} The Swiss-relevant subset of European Court of Human Rights jurisprudence (\textbf{2{,}788 decisions}: 1{,}153 chamber + 93 grand-chamber + 230 committee + 835 HUDOC entries + 477 BGE-EGMR mirror) is integrated as a first-class federation. The set is refreshed daily; on the snapshot date the most recent ingest carried a same-day Article 11 violation against Switzerland (application 30781/22, decided 2026-05-07). The subset matters because \textbf{Article 6~ECHR} is the second-most-cited statutory provision in our graph (after federal-court procedure), and the official versions of ECHR-Switzerland judgments are typically French and English, requiring a multilingual retrieval substrate.

\paragraph{Completeness.} A persistent challenge for Swiss legal corpora is that several cantonal portals geo-block or rate-limit research scrapers. We document and release the recovery techniques in our reproducibility manifest, including a reverse-SOCKS tunnel via residential IP for Hetzner-blocked portals (JU, NE) and Wayback Machine recovery for closed historical archives (BL, $\sim$903 ALS judgments).

\paragraph{Licensing.} The base corpus is released under CC-BY-4.0; commentaries from OnlineKommentar.ch are CC-BY; from OpenLegalCommentary.ch CC-BY-SA. Decision IDs are stable across daily refreshes.

\section{Resource: knowledge graph}\label{sec:graph}

\input{tables/citation_graph.tex}
\input{tables/cross_lang_citation_matrix.tex}

\paragraph{Citation graph.} We extract citation edges by full-text scanning every decision in the corpus (\textbf{971{,}732} decisions in the reference-graph snapshot, aligned with the FTS5 search index), recovering \textbf{8{,}648{,}462 distinct outgoing references} of which \textbf{8{,}033{,}289 (92.9\,\%)} resolve to a target decision in the corpus. Resolution proceeds in three exact-match passes (general docket-normalisation; BGE/ATF/DTF prefix-form; bare BGE numeric form), augmented by a pin-cite-aware fallback for Swiss legal citations such as \emph{BGE 125 V 352} that pinpoint into the body of a case starting at \emph{BGE 125 V 351}. The pin-cite resolver matches each unresolved BGE-form reference to the nearest preceding first-page in the same volume\,$+$\,division within 30 pages, contributing \textbf{652{,}283} additional matches at a $-$0.10 confidence discount versus exact matches. The remaining 615{,}173 unresolved pairs (7.1\,\%) are dominated by cantonal or lower-court references genuinely outside our current coverage; the residual is BGE volumes we do not hold and malformed extraction artefacts (see \texttt{benchmarks/citation\_resolution\_analysis.py}). The deployed 92.9\,\% resolution is a 17.7\,pp lift over the prior release's 75.2\,\%, recovered by fixing two implementation bugs: docket-normalisation drift in 2007--2009 \emph{Bundesgericht} dockets stored with a space (\texttt{8C 862\_2008}) versus the underscore form (\texttt{8C\_862\_2008}) emitted by the citation extractor, and the pin-cite mismatch above.

The citation graph is heavy-tailed (Table~\ref{tab:citation_graph}): 40 decisions are cited 10{,}000+ times, 933 are cited 1{,}000--9{,}999 times, and the rest follow a power law. The single most-cited decision is BGE 125 V 351 (66{,}809 incoming citations), the foundational case on the evidentiary weight of party-commissioned medical reports in social-insurance litigation.

\paragraph{Cross-lingual citation.} Table~\ref{tab:cross_lang_citation} reports the language-by-language citation matrix. \textbf{34.0\,\% of all resolved citations are cross-lingual}: French decisions cite German decisions \textbf{1.94M} times (FR$\to$DE dominates the off-diagonal), German decisions cite French decisions \textbf{0.46M} times, and Italian decisions cite German decisions \textbf{0.17M} times. The asymmetry --- DE$\leftrightarrow$FR is bidirectional but FR$\to$DE outweighs DE$\to$FR by 4.2$\times$, while IT$\to$DE/FR (0.24M combined) dominates IT$\to$IT (37k) by $\sim$6$\times$ --- reflects practitioners' practical reliance on German jurisprudence as binding precedent in mixed-language litigation.

\paragraph{Statute graph.} We resolve \textbf{11{,}260{,}080 decision-to-statute edges} touching \textbf{283{,}119 distinct provisions}. The statute layer is grounded in two parallel mirrors: a federal layer of \textbf{5{,}516 SR-numbered Fedlex laws} (400{,}405 article rows summed across the parallel DE/FR/IT versions; $\sim$133\,k articles per language) and a cantonal layer of \textbf{15{,}722 laws} (353{,}437 articles) covering all 26 cantons --- 22 via direct portal scraping (LexWork, SIL, ZH OpenData, TI~RL; 12{,}177 laws) plus 4 via LexFind PDF fallback (JU, SZ, UR, VD; 3{,}545 laws). Top-referenced provisions are dominated by federal-court procedural code: \emph{Bundesgerichtsgesetz} Art.~42 (motivation requirement, $\geq$200\,k references when LTF and BGG abbreviation forms are pooled), Art.~100 (deadline), and \emph{ATSG/LPGA} Art.~61 (social-insurance procedure).

\paragraph{Materialien graph (Federal Council Botschaften).} A distinguishing feature of Swiss civil-law interpretation is the \emph{teleological} reading of statutes via \emph{Materialien} --- legislative-history documents (Federal Council messages, parliamentary debates) that record legislative intent. We discover Materialien via the Fedlex JOLUX SPARQL endpoint (\texttt{typeDocument} = 23) and ingest them as \textbf{5{,}292 Botschaft documents} (DE 1{,}763, FR 1{,}765, IT 1{,}764, ranging 2003--2026), parsed at paragraph granularity (\textbf{381{,}711 paragraphs}) from Akoma Ntoso XML or PDF fallback. We anchor paragraphs to specific \texttt{(SR, article)} pairs by traversing the parliament-chain (\texttt{parliamentDraftId} $\to$ enacted \texttt{oc} $\to$ codified \texttt{cc} $\to$ SR), yielding \textbf{8{,}124 article-to-Botschaft links} (DE 2{,}798, FR 2{,}742, IT 2{,}584). The Materialien layer is queryable via \texttt{search\_materialien} / \texttt{get\_materialien} MCP tools and surfaces verbatim legislative-intent text under any cited statute article. To our knowledge this is the first open, machine-queryable bridge between the Swiss case-law corpus and the Botschaften that a court would consult in interpretation.

\section{Infrastructure}\label{sec:infra}

The corpus, graph, statutes, and Materialien are served through four production surfaces:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MCP server} (\texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch/sse}, public SSE). 31 tools partitioned into four families: \emph{retrieval} (\texttt{search\_decisions}, \texttt{get\_decision}, \texttt{find\_leading\_cases}, \texttt{find\_citations}, \texttt{find\_appeal\_chain}, \texttt{search\_laws}, \texttt{get\_law}, \texttt{search\_legislation}, \texttt{get\_legislation}, \texttt{browse\_legislation\_changes}, \texttt{search\_commentaries}, \texttt{get\_commentary}, \texttt{search\_materialien}, \texttt{get\_materialien}, \texttt{search\_practice}, \texttt{get\_practice}); \emph{analysis} (\texttt{analyze\_legal\_trend}, \texttt{get\_doctrine}, \texttt{get\_decision\_structure}, \texttt{get\_erwaegung}, \texttt{find\_relevant\_erwaegung}, \texttt{get\_regeste}, \texttt{get\_article\_purpose}, \texttt{get\_case\_brief}, \texttt{get\_statistics}, \texttt{list\_courts}); \emph{generation} (\texttt{generate\_exam\_question}, \texttt{draft\_mock\_decision}); and \emph{verification} (\texttt{check\_claim\_support}, \texttt{attest\_response}, \texttt{cite}). Two additional tools (\texttt{update\_database}, \texttt{check\_update\_status}) are local-only and hidden by the \texttt{REMOTE\_MODE} filter on the public deployment.
\item \textbf{REST API} (FastAPI; export endpoints \texttt{.docx / .pdf / .bib / .ris} per decision; OpenAPI spec at \texttt{/api/openapi.copilot.json} curated for Microsoft Copilot Studio adoption).
\item \textbf{Word add-in} (\texttt{opencaselaw.ch/word}, public). Lawyer-facing client that surfaces the verification rails as inline citations, with structural client+server PII redaction at boundary.
\item \textbf{Public dashboards} at \url{https://opencaselaw.ch} (corpus stats, multilingual landing) and \url{https://opencaselaw.ch/quality.html} (live dataset health).
\end{itemize}

The retrieval substrate is a SQLite FTS5 index ($\sim$63\,GB on disk). The nightly refresh uses an \emph{atomic-rebuild} pattern: the new index is constructed in a sibling \texttt{.db.tmp} file, then promoted via \texttt{os.replace}; live readers (4 uvicorn workers behind nginx) are never interrupted.

\paragraph{Adoption signal.} The MCP endpoint has registered early integration from a Swiss-French law firm (Lalive, via Microsoft Copilot Studio) and from a cantonal-government legal department (\'Etat du Valais, custom MCP client), with sustained query volume in legal-research patterns rather than synthetic load. Adoption is descriptive, not an evaluation claim, but it confirms that the deployed stack is exercised by professionals on real research questions.

\section{Five-rail closing audit}\label{sec:audit}

We deploy a five-rail closing audit on every LLM response generated by the public MCP. Each rail addresses a single named error class: (R1)~case-citation existence, (R2)~statute reference resolution, (R3)~verbatim-quote source matching, (R4)~decision-date sanity, and (R5)~LLM-judge proposition grounding. R1--R4 are deterministic regex+lookup ($\sim$3\,ms total), R5 is judge-mediated ($\sim$2\,s, $\sim$\$0.005). The decomposition by error class lets each rail be tested, ablated, and improved in isolation.

\section{Cross-lingual leading-case retrieval}\label{sec:crosslang}

\input{tables/baseline_by_language.tex}
\input{tables/cross_lingual_mrr.tex}
\input{tables/cross_lingual_hit.tex}

\paragraph{Setup.} We select 50 leading cases from the most-cited subset of the corpus (in-degree range $4{,}412$--$53{,}251$, median $8{,}180$), stratified to at most four cases per legal area to ensure diversity across the 22 covered areas (constitutional, social-insurance, federal-court procedure, criminal, civil-procedure, contract, tax, asylum, and more). All 50 cases lie in the top 0.025\,\% of distinct cited decisions in the corpus by in-degree. For each case we extract one query per language (DE, FR, IT) from terms in that language's official regeste, after dropping article-reference noise (e.g.\ \texttt{Art.\ 6 Abs.\ 1 UVG}) and language-specific stop words. The query construction is keyword-list style, not a free natural-language paraphrase; we discuss the realism implications in Section~\ref{sec:limit}. The dataset is released as \texttt{benchmarks/swiss\_legal\_rag\_bench/cross\_lingual\_v1.jsonl} (CC0). Of the 50 cases, 38 are originally German and 12 originally French --- consistent with the corpus distribution among most-cited decisions. No selected case is originally Italian; we treat this as a coverage gap (Section~\ref{sec:limit}).

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:cross_lingual_mrr} reports MRR@10 and Table~\ref{tab:cross_lingual_hit} reports Hit@10 per (Q lang, T lang) cell. Overall MRR@10$=$0.630, Hit@10$=$0.833 (125 of 150 queries place the target in the top 10).

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Italian queries retrieve German targets best (MRR$=$0.776).} Italian queries outperform German queries on the \emph{same} 38 German targets (DE$\to$DE MRR$=$0.617). One mechanism: Italian regestes carry distinctive shared markers (\emph{DTF}, \emph{cpv.}, \emph{LAINF}) that anchor retrieval, while German queries compete with the broader German subset (449\,k decisions, the largest stratum) and so retrieve more in-language distractors.
\item \textbf{Same-language French is the strongest cell (MRR$=$0.785, Hit@10$=$1.0).} For the 12 French-original leading cases, French queries place the target in the top 10 every time and at rank 1 in 67\,\% of cases.
\item \textbf{German-to-French is the hardest cell (MRR$=$0.333, Hit@10$=$0.333).} German queries struggle to retrieve French-original BGEs because the discriminative German terms in the regeste-derived queries do not appear in the French-language full text and the BM25+RRF baseline does no cross-lingual translation.
\item \textbf{Diagonal vs.\ off-diagonal gap is small.} Weighted across n, same-language diagonal cells (DE$\to$DE n$=$38, FR$\to$FR n$=$12) score MRR$=$0.657; the four off-diagonal cells (n$=$100 total) score MRR$=$0.616. The 0.04 gap shows that cross-lingual retrieval against a multilingual leading-case index is feasible without translation, although the cell-by-cell variance (0.333 to 0.776) is large.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hallucination stress test}\label{sec:hallu}

We evaluate the five-rail audit on the v2 30-question Swiss Legal RAG bench~\cite{butler2026}, which spans 17 substantive areas of Swiss federal law (Schuldrecht, Strafrecht, Verfassungsrecht, Verfahrensrecht, Mietrecht, Verjährung, ...). Language distribution: DE$=$18, FR$=$8, IT$=$4 (60\,/\,27\,/\,13\,\%). Each question is paired with a corpus-anchored reference answer (statute SR-number plus article number plus language; leading-case \texttt{decision\_id}(s) where applicable). 16 of 30 questions are anchored on a verified leading-case BGE; 14 carry the \texttt{doctrine\_from\_bge} claim type.

\paragraph{Conditions evaluated.} \emph{Prior-only}: the generator answers from training prior alone, no retrieval. We deliberately use the prior-only condition as a worst-case stress test that maximises errors; a retrieval-augmented condition is deferred to v1.1 of the benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:limit}). The audit pipeline is condition-agnostic: the rails make no assumption about whether a draft was produced with or without retrieval, so the same deployment audits both.

\paragraph{Findings (prior-only).} Across 30 prior-only drafts containing $\sim$33 distinct authority citations, the deterministic rails (R1--R4) flagged exactly 1 unresolvable citation: a 3\,\% \emph{observed} fabrication rate. For comparison, Magesh et al.~\cite{magesh2024} report 17--33\,\% on commercial legal-RAG products and Dahl et al.~\cite{dahl2024} report 58--82\,\% on raw general-purpose LLMs (different conditions, different jurisdictions, not directly comparable; we cite to bracket the literature). The single failing docket (BGE/DTF 117 II 198) fails consistently across re-runs: with our deterministic rails alone we cannot distinguish a true fabrication from a corpus gap without manual adjudication. The full five-rail audit including the LLM-judge rail reaches a 66.7\,\% wrong-draft flag rate at a 50\,\% false-positive rate; calibration of the judge's threshold remains an open problem and the rail should currently be deployed in advisory mode rather than as a hard binary gate.

\paragraph{Retrieval-augmented condition.} A side-by-side retrieval-augmented re-run is the highest-priority follow-on for v1.1. We expect retrieval to substantially reduce R1 fabrication-existence flags (the system retrieves real authorities rather than relying on prior); the open question is whether judge-rail false-positive rate increases (more authorities to ground against) or decreases (clearer source pool). We commit to publish the retrieval-augmented numbers as a versioned update on the benchmark's Hugging Face page rather than holding the resource paper's release on it.

\section{Dataset health and drift}\label{sec:qc}

OpenCaseLaw operates a four-layer quality framework:
\begin{description}
\item[L1 Function tests] (46 pytest cases, every CI push, $<$90\,s).
\item[L2 Pipeline guards] (5 codified \texttt{\_normalize\_*} passes in \texttt{build\_fts5.py}, every nightly rebuild).
\item[L3 Dataset audit] (61 \texttt{check\_*} functions across 17 axes: schema, dates, dockets, courts, languages, regeste coverage, URL integrity, duplicates, text quality, structure, cross-DB consistency, citation graph, statute graph, floors, per-court drift, exports round-trip, MCP-tool round-trip).
\item[L4 Production smoke] (every 5\,min via systemd timer; 6 endpoint probes; $p_{95}<2$\,s).
\end{description}

A dataset-audit run that detects a critical regression \emph{blocks the nightly publish}: the orchestrator (\texttt{publish.py}) refuses to advance to the git-push step and instead emits an urgent ntfy alert. Critical conditions include a total-count cliff $>$1\,\%, schema break, EGMR re-regression, court not in registry, future date count $>$50, cross-DB row-count mismatch $>$0.5\,\%. Warning conditions (per-court drift $>$10\,\% from 7-day median, OCR-quality regression, citation-graph edge drop $>$5\,\%) emit alerts but do not block publish.

\section{Discussion and generalisation}\label{sec:disc}

The cross-lingual results have a clear takeaway for legal-RAG practitioners: \emph{retrieval quality is asymmetric across languages and direction}. A naive deployment that only validates same-language MRR will overestimate user-facing quality in mixed-language jurisdictions. Our public benchmark allows quantitative pre-deployment audits of cross-lingual performance.

The verification framework generalises beyond Switzerland. Each rail of the closing audit is corpus-agnostic given (a) an enumerable decision corpus, (b) a statute mirror, and (c) an extraction grammar for the local citation conventions. We sketch an English-law application in Appendix~\ref{app:english}. The Materialien layer is the most directly Switzerland-specific contribution: jurisdictions with comparable legislative-history doctrine (Germany's \emph{Gesetzgebungsmaterialien}, Canada's \emph{Hansard}, the United States' \emph{legislative history}) could replicate the parliament-chain SPARQL traversal pattern over their own gazette ontologies.

The early-adoption signal --- a Swiss-French law firm and a cantonal-government legal department actively querying the deployed MCP within weeks of soft launch --- suggests that the resource fills a deployment-blocker gap rather than only a research-evaluation gap. The corollary risk: any infrastructure regression now affects production legal research, which motivates the four-layer QC framework (Section~\ref{sec:qc}) as a non-negotiable accompaniment to release.

\section{Limitations and ethics}\label{sec:limit}

\paragraph{Benchmark scope.} The cross-lingual question set is single-curator and limited to 50 cases (150 trials). It is anchored on the citation graph (target decisions are objectively-ranked leading cases) but the queries themselves are programmatic keyword extractions from the corresponding language's regeste, not free legal-research paraphrases. v2.0 of the benchmark will recruit multi-annotator panels to reach inter-annotator agreement.

\paragraph{Query--target coupling (upper-bound caveat).} Because each query is derived from the same case's official multilingual regeste, the query and target share distinctive vocabulary the retrieval system can latch onto. A real legal-research query, written without sight of the regeste, may not contain those discriminators. We therefore read the absolute MRR/Hit@10 numbers as an \emph{upper bound} on realistic retrieval performance, and the cross-cell ranking (which cells are easier or harder, by how much) as the more reliable signal. v2.0 will replace regeste-derived queries with held-out, lawyer-authored research questions.

\paragraph{Italian-target coverage.} No top-50 case is originally Italian (top-50 is dominated by social-insurance and procedural BGEs that originate in the German-language stream). This is consistent with the corpus distribution among most-cited decisions but is a coverage gap; v2.0 will explicitly include Italian-original cases sampled at lower citation centrality.

\paragraph{Privacy.} Court decisions are public; published BGE/cantonal decisions are typically pseudonymised. We do not republish parties' private identifying information beyond what the courts themselves publish.

\paragraph{Calibration.} The grounding-judge rail (R5) is a judge-mediated LLM call; we report a 41.7\,\% false-positive rate on correct drafts in our v2 paper. Full-binary deployment would over-flag; we recommend the rail be used in advisory mode pending v2.0 calibration work.
