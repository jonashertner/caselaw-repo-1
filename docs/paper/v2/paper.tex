\begin{abstract}
Peer-reviewed measurement (Dahl et al., 2024; Magesh et al., 2024)
finds that 58--82\,\% of legal queries to general-purpose LLMs and
17--33\,\% to commercial legal-RAG tools produce a fabricated
authority.  We present a five-rail closing audit pipeline for
retrieval-augmented legal LLMs that addresses each error class with
the cheapest mechanism that can detect it.  The rails are:
case-citation existence, statute-reference resolution,
verbatim-quote source-matching, decision-date sanity, and
proposition grounding by an independent LLM judge.  Four rails are
deterministic (${\sim}3$\,ms per draft); the fifth is judge-mediated
(${\sim}2$\,s, ${\approx}\$0.005$ per call).  We release \textbf{Swiss
Legal RAG Bench v0.2}, the first multilingual benchmark for legal-RAG
\emph{evaluation} on Swiss federal law (DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT, 30
expert-annotated questions, every BGE evidence anchor verified
against the live corpus before publication).  Evaluating Claude
Sonnet 4.6 in a prior-only condition (no retrieval), we measure: (i)
\textbf{3.0\,\%} citation-level fabrication --- well below Magesh's
17--33\,\% on commercial legal-RAG tools, attributable to Swiss
corpus presence in the model's pre-training; and (ii) the residual
error class is not citation fabrication but \emph{real citation +
misrepresented holding}, where the model cites a real BGE correctly
but omits its load-bearing precedential element.  The four
deterministic rails catch this class at 16.7\,\% recall (and 0\,\%
false-positive rate); the LLM-judge rail extends recall to 50\,\%
at 25\,\% FPR.  Closing this residual gap is the open problem for
verification-first legal RAG.  Code MIT, benchmark CC0; both
positioned as seeds for community-contributed expansion to v1.0
(150 questions, named expert co-authors).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Legal practice is unusually unforgiving of confident-sounding
incorrect answers.  A lawyer who cites a fabricated case, miscites a
statute, or attaches a false holding to a real authority risks
professional discipline and a wrong outcome for the client; in
Switzerland, this is a violation of the \emph{Sorgfaltspflicht}
(duty of care) under the federal \emph{Anwaltsgesetz} (BGFA, SR
935.61, art.~12).  As large language models become a routine part of
legal research, this asymmetry collides with a measured failure
mode: 58--82\,\% of legal queries to general-purpose LLMs (Dahl et
al., 2024) and 17--33\,\% of queries to commercial legal-RAG products
(Magesh et al., 2024) produce a fabricated authority, miscited
statute, or unsupported quotation.

Two recent developments shape the present work.  First, Butler \&
Butler (2026) introduce a clean methodology for evaluating legal-RAG
systems: decompose each error into three orthogonal binary signals
(correctness $c_{eli}$, groundedness $g_{eli}$, retrieval accuracy
$r_{eli}$) and read off the failure class from their joint
distribution.  Their methodology lets us ask \emph{which} rail of a
verification system addresses \emph{which} error class.  Second, the
Swiss legal NLP literature has matured rapidly: SCALE (Rasiah et
al., 2023) introduced a multi-task Swiss legal benchmark suite, the
Niklaus group released anonymization tooling (Niklaus, Mami{\'e} et
al., 2023) and re-identification risk measurement (Nyffenegger,
St{\"u}rmer \& Niklaus, 2024) for Swiss court rulings, SwiLTra-Bench
(Niklaus et al., 2025) provided 180\,k aligned Swiss legal
translation pairs, and the SLDS dataset (Rolshoven, Rasiah et al.,
2025) offered 20\,k cross-lingual SFSC summarisation pairs.  None of
these target retrieval-augmented \emph{generation} as the unit of
evaluation; the present paper occupies that specific gap.

\paragraph{Two findings, in tension, motivate the rail decomposition.}
Our central empirical result is that on a closed Swiss-law corpus
(969\,k decisions, daily refresh, in the model's pre-training)
Claude Sonnet 4.6 fabricates only \textbf{3.0\,\%} of emitted
citations --- an order of magnitude below the Magesh et al.\ (2024)
17--33\,\% measurement on commercial legal-RAG products.  At face
value this would suggest verification is unnecessary.  But the
$c_{eli}$ correctness rate is only 80\,\% (24/30): of the 6 wrong
drafts, only 1 contains a fabricated citation; the remaining 5
stem from a different mechanism --- the model cites a \emph{real}
BGE \emph{correctly} but \emph{misrepresents} the holding,
omitting the load-bearing precedential element or attributing a
different doctrine to the case.  Per-citation existence checks cannot detect this; an
LLM-judge grounding rail extends recall but at meaningful
false-positive cost.  This residual class is what motivates the
defense-in-depth design that follows.

\paragraph{The conceptual move.}
A production verification system for legal RAG should not be a
single black-box LLM judge over the final draft, nor a single
per-citation retrieval check, but a \textbf{decomposition into
independent rails, one per named error class}, each implemented with
the cheapest mechanism that can detect that class.  The decomposition
lets each rail be tested, ablated, and improved in isolation, and
lets every draft pay only for the rails it needs.  We instantiate
the design as a five-rail closing audit pipeline
(\S\ref{sec:rails}) deployed in production at
\url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A defense-in-depth closing audit pipeline for legal RAG,
        with five rails addressing the four Magesh\,/\,Dahl error
        classes plus a fifth (date confabulation).  Latency
        $\leq 3$\,ms for the four deterministic rails;
        ${\sim}2$\,s, ${\approx}\$0.005$ per call for the LLM-judge
        rail.
  \item \textbf{Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2}: the first multilingual
        \emph{retrieval-augmented-generation} benchmark for Swiss
        federal law (DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT, 30 expert-annotated questions),
        modelled on Butler \& Butler (2026) with cross-lingual
        retrieval added as a fifth evaluation dimension.  Released
        as a seed; v1.0 (150 questions, named expert co-authors)
        is the planned gold-standard deliverable.
  \item Per-rail ablation identifying the residual error class
        --- \emph{real citation, misrepresented holding} --- as
        the open problem for verification-first legal RAG.  Even
        the LLM-judge rail catches it at only 2/3.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Legal-LLM hallucination measurement.}
Dahl et al.\ (2024, \emph{Large Legal Fictions}) profiled
hallucination on six legal-task types in general-purpose LLMs,
reporting fabricated-authority rates of 58--82\,\%.  The follow-up
Magesh et al.\ (2024, \emph{Hallucination-Free?}) measured
commercial legal-RAG tools (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research,
Ask Practical Law) at 17--33\,\%.  Our paper accepts these as the
problem statement; the five-rail pipeline of \S\ref{sec:rails} is
the treatment.

\paragraph{Legal-RAG benchmarks.}
Butler \& Butler (2026, \emph{Legal RAG Bench}) introduced the
four-leaf error decomposition methodology we extend.  Their
benchmark covers Australian criminal law (4{,}876 passages from the
Victorian Criminal Charge Book, 100 hand-crafted questions) and uses
a closed corpus.  We adapt the methodology to Swiss multilingual
federal law, adding cross-language retrieval as a fifth leaf because
Switzerland is the only major jurisdiction where the authoritative
version of a leading case may be in a language other than the
question's.  Closed-domain multiple-choice benchmarks
(LegalBench-RAG, Pipitone \& Houir Alami 2024; HousingQA /
BarExamQA, Zheng et al.\ 2025; LegalBench, Guha et al.\ 2023) do not
test hallucination because the model never needs to invent an
answer.

\paragraph{Swiss legal NLP.}
Recent Swiss legal NLP has matured rapidly around the Niklaus /
St{\"u}rmer / Stanford / UZH / Bern axis.  Three lines are
directly adjacent to ours.  (i) \emph{Privacy and anonymisation}:
Niklaus, Mami{\'e} et al.\ (2023) released BERT-based models for
entity-level anonymisation of Swiss FSCS rulings, improving the
operational \emph{Anom2} system used by the Federal Supreme Court;
Nyffenegger, St{\"u}rmer \& Niklaus (2024) systematically measured
LLM-based re-identification risk on anonymised Swiss court rulings,
finding the risk minimal for top models on \emph{court decisions}
(higher on Wikipedia entities).  We discuss the implications for
our corpus exposure in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.  (ii) \emph{Multilingual
benchmarks}: SwiLTra-Bench (Niklaus et al., 2025) is a 180\,k-pair
Swiss legal translation benchmark across DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT\,/\,RM\,/\,EN,
introducing the SwiLTra-Judge specialised LLM evaluator with
human-expert validation.  SLDS (Rolshoven, Rasiah et al., 2025)
provides 20\,k SFSC rulings with cross-lingual headnote
summarisations.  We do not duplicate these tasks; our cross-lingual
test (q-028) targets retrieval, not translation or summarisation.
(iii) \emph{Multi-task suites}: SCALE (Rasiah et al., 2023) covers
citation extraction, court-view generation and summarisation;
Swiss-Judgment-Prediction (Niklaus, Chalkidis \& St{\"u}rmer, 2021)
addresses outcome prediction; MultiLegalPile (Niklaus et al., 2023)
provides a 689\,GB multilingual legal pre-training corpus.  None of
these target retrieval-augmented \emph{generation evaluation}; the
present paper occupies that specific gap.

\paragraph{Verification methods.}
Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023) and CRAG (Yan et al., 2024) propose
general-domain self-verification or retrieval-correction; both treat
verification as \emph{pre-generation} (judge passage quality before
generating).  Our pipeline is \emph{post-generation} verification:
parse the generated draft, audit each emitted reference against the
closed corpus, return a verdict per rail.  The decomposition by
named error class is, to our knowledge, novel for legal RAG; it is
analogous in spirit to the SwiLTra-Judge calibration approach
(Niklaus et al., 2025) but addresses retrieval-grounded text
generation rather than translation evaluation.

\paragraph{Positioning.}  No prior work proposes a \emph{deployed}
mechanism that systematically reduces hallucination by decomposing
the verification problem into independent error-class detectors.
Existing RAG systems either treat verification as a single
LLM-judge pass over the final output (which inherits the same
model's biases) or as per-citation retrieval validation (which
catches existence errors but not propositional mis-grounding).  The
five-rail closing audit we propose in \S\ref{sec:rails} is the
first to address each error class with the cheapest mechanism that
can detect it, with each rail independently testable and
ablatable.

\section{The Five-Rail Audit Pipeline}
\label{sec:rails}

The audit operates on a draft answer $D$ and a retrieval-state
pool $S$ (decisions and statutes the generator has access to).  It
returns a list of issues ${\cal I}(D, S)$, each labelled with one
of five categories (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).  An $\textsc{ok}$
verdict (no issues) means the audit found nothing; $\neg\textsc{ok}$
means at least one rail fired.  The five rails operate
\emph{in parallel} on the same draft.

\paragraph{Rail 1 --- case-citation existence.}
A regex parses the draft for Swiss case-citation forms
(BGE / ATF / DTF, BGer / TF dockets, BVGer / BStGer /
BPatGer dockets, MKGE numerical references).  Each
parsed citation is resolved against the corpus by
exact-match lookup with a strict canonical-prefix guard;
the LIKE-fallback path is suppressed for inputs that
look like canonical decision IDs because such inputs
either hit the indexed exact-match path or are
fabricated.  Latency: 2--5\,ms per draft regardless of
citation count.  Catches: invented BGE numbers, mis-typed
docket numbers, citations to retracted decisions.
Optional pinpoint check: if the citation includes
$\text{E.}\,n.m$ or $\text{consid.}\,n.m$, the pinpoint
must match either the structured-extraction sidecar or
the body-text \texttt{regeste} of the cited decision.

\paragraph{Rail 2 --- statute-reference resolution.}
A second regex parses for $\text{Art.}\,N\,\textsc{LAW}$
forms (with German\,/\,French\,/\,Italian abbreviation
synonyms) and resolves each against the Fedlex SPARQL
mirror cached locally as \texttt{statutes.db}.  The
resolution is two-step --- abbreviation $\rightarrow$ SR
number $\rightarrow$ article text --- and is cached at
process scope.  Latency: 1--3\,ms per draft.  Catches:
fabricated law abbreviations, wrong article numbers,
references to repealed articles.  Connector words
(``und'', ``oder'', ``et'', ``ou'') are case-sensitively
filtered to prevent the regex eating them as fake law
codes after $\text{Abs.}\,N$ structures.

\paragraph{Rail 3 --- verbatim-quote source-matching.}
Quoted substrings of $\geq 30$ characters in the draft
are extracted (German „...\textquotedblleft, French
\guillemotleft\,...\,\guillemotright, Italian
\guillemotleft\,...\,\guillemotright, English curly
``\,\dots\,'', ASCII ``...'') and required to appear
verbatim --- after whitespace + quote-mark normalisation
--- in the source pool $S$.  The pool comprises the
\texttt{regeste} text plus first 8\,k characters of
each cited decision, plus the article text of each
cited statute.  Latency: $\sim$2\,ms.  Catches:
hallucinated quotations, paraphrases inside quotation
marks.

\paragraph{Rail 4 --- decision-date sanity.}
For each verified case citation, parse adjacent
$\text{vom}\,/\,du\,/\,del\,$ DD.MM.YYYY constructs
within 60 characters of the citation span and compare
to the stored \texttt{decision\_date}.  Catches: model
emits a real BGE but pairs it with a wrong date,
suggesting confusion between two cases with similar
dockets.  Latency: $<\!1$\,ms.

\paragraph{Rail 5 --- proposition grounding (opt-in).}
For each verified citation, the immediately preceding
claim sentence is extracted (up to 250 characters
before the citation, terminated by sentence boundary;
``see''-cite markers ``vgl.'' / ``siehe'' / ``cf.'' /
``cfr.'' suppress the rail).  All (claim, source) pairs
go in a \emph{single batched} prompt to an independent
Sonnet judge that returns a JSON array of verdicts
$\{$\texttt{yes, partial, no, contradicts,
unrelated}$\}$.  Verdicts in
$\{$\texttt{no, contradicts, unrelated}$\}$ become
issues.  One Sonnet call per draft regardless of
citation count.  Latency: 1.5--2.5\,s.  Cost:
$\approx\$0.005$ per call.  Catches: real citation
+ misrepresented holding (the Butler\,/\,Butler
``reasoning error'' class).

\paragraph{Output canonicalisation.}
When all rails pass, the audit emits a
\texttt{linked\_text} field with every parsed
citation replaced by its corpus-canonical form
wrapped in a Markdown link to the source URL on
\texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch/entscheid/<id>}.  Drafts
arrive with messy citation spacing or case and
leave publication-ready.

\section{Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2}
\label{sec:benchmark}

We release Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2 alongside this
paper.  Version 0.2 contains 30 expert-annotated
questions across the major substantive areas of Swiss
federal law (Schuldrecht, Sachenrecht, Familienrecht,
Erbrecht, Strafrecht, Strafprozess, Verfassungsrecht,
Verwaltungsrecht, Mietrecht, Arbeitsrecht,
Sozialversicherungsrecht, Verkehrsrecht, Aktienrecht,
SchKG, Datenschutz, Verjährung, Wirtschaftsfreiheit;
17 distinct areas).  The language distribution is
DE\,=\,18, FR\,=\,8, IT\,=\,4 (60\,/\,27\,/\,13\,\%).

\paragraph{Question construction.}
Each question is paired with a concise reference answer
(1--4 sentences in the question's language) and
annotated supporting evidence (statute SR-number
$+$ article number $+$ language; leading-case
\texttt{decision\_id}\,(s) where applicable).  Of the
30 questions, 16 (53\,\%) are anchored on a verified
leading-case BGE; 14 (47\,\%) carry the
\texttt{doctrine\_from\_bge} claim type, in which the
expected answer derives a doctrinal proposition from
a seed BGE rather than restating its holding directly.
The two annotations are not disjoint: every
\texttt{doctrine\_from\_bge} question is also anchored
on a leading-case BGE.  The split exists to let the
ablation surface where each rail contributes ---
case-existence on the seed citations, the grounding
judge on the doctrinal proposition.

\paragraph{Corpus verification of evidence.}
Every \texttt{decision\_id} cited as evidence was
resolved against the live corpus
(\url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch/api/decisions/<id>})
before the benchmark was published; every BGE
returned a 200 response with non-zero citation count
and an on-topic \texttt{regeste}.  This is itself a
methodological discipline: most legal-RAG benchmarks
build evidence annotations from secondary sources and
inherit any errors there.  We commit to corpus-first
construction, with the audit trail published in
the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Cross-lingual extension.}
Switzerland is the only major civil-law jurisdiction
where the authoritative version of a leading case may
be in a language other than the question's.  Question
q-028 (IT) anchors on DTF 126 III 113 (which has
regesti in DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT) to test the
cross-language retrieval claim.  v1.0 expands this
dimension to a full cross-language retrieval suite.

\paragraph{Versioning toward gold standard.}
v0.2 is a seed.  The version we expect to reach the
``gold standard'' bar for Swiss legal practice is
\textbf{v1.0}, targeted at 150 questions with named
expert co-authors from Swiss law faculties.  v0.2
allows the present paper to characterise the audit
pipeline; we explicitly invite contributors for v1.0.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We use Claude Sonnet 4.6 (released 2026-04) as both the
generator and the judge for $c_{eli}$ and $g_{eli}$
scoring.  Same model used both sides is a known
limitation (\S\ref{sec:discussion}); we use one model
because the paper's contribution is the architecture,
not the cost-optimisation.  All 30 questions in v0.2
are run in a \emph{prior-only} condition (no
retrieval): the generator sees only the question
text and must rely on training knowledge.  The
prior-only condition is methodologically clean for
the ablation: it guarantees the rails have something
to catch and is the closest replication of Dahl et
al.\ 2024's general-LLM measurement.

For each question we collect: the prior-only draft,
the per-category issue counts from
\texttt{attest\_response} with all five rails on,
the $c_{eli}$ judge verdict against the v0.2 reference
answer, and the $g_{eli}$ judge verdict against the
empty source pool.  $g_{eli}$ is uniformly $0$ in the
prior-only condition by construction (no retrieved
sources $\Rightarrow$ no claim can be grounded).
We use the $c_{eli}$ verdict as ground truth for
the per-rail ablation.

Per question we issue exactly four Sonnet calls (one
generation, one in-pipeline grounding judge, one
$c_{eli}$ judge, one $g_{eli}$ judge) plus one
deterministic-rails pass; the 6-row ablation in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} is computed in
post-processing by intersecting each draft's
per-rail fire flags with each configuration's
enabled rail set.  Total: 120 Sonnet calls across
30 drafts; 106\,s wall-clock with 4-way parallelism;
${<\!}\$2$ in API charges.

\paragraph{Citation-level fabrication rate.}
Across 30 prior-only drafts, Claude Sonnet 4.6 emits
33 case citations of which 32 resolve in the corpus.
The citation-level fabrication rate is
\textbf{3.0\,\%}, well below Magesh et al.'s 17--33\,\%
on commercial legal-RAG tools and Dahl et al.'s
58--82\,\% on general LLMs.  We attribute the gap to
corpus presence in the model's pre-training: Swiss
BGE references appear at scale on the open web (and
in our published Hugging Face dataset), and the
model has internalised the citation conventions.
This finding is jurisdiction- and model-specific:
we do not claim the same rate would obtain for
\emph{any} model on \emph{any} legal corpus.

\paragraph{Per-rail ablation.}
Of the 30 prior-only drafts, 24 are judged
correct ($c_{eli} = 1$) and 6 wrong ($c_{eli} = 0$)
by the independent Sonnet judge.  Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
reports the catch rate of each cumulative
configuration on the wrong-draft subset (true
positives) and the correct-draft subset (false
positives).

Two findings emerge.  First, the deterministic rails
(\textsc{case} through \textsc{date}) catch
fabrications with \emph{perfect precision} (0\,\% FPR
at $C_1$) but limited recall (16.7\,\% TPR).  The
single fabrication they catch is q-017's invented
DTF 117 II 198, which Sonnet uses to anchor an
otherwise-correct paraphrase of art.~264 CO; our
case-existence rail flags it cleanly.  Second, the
LLM-judge grounding rail extends recall to 50\,\% but
at 25\,\% FPR --- six correct drafts trigger the rail
because the judge's batched call disagrees with the
draft's interpretation of a real cited authority.

\paragraph{The residual error class.}
Of the 6 wrong drafts, 3 are caught by some rail and
3 are missed by all.  The 3 missed drafts share a
pattern: Sonnet cites a \emph{real} BGE
(BGE 142 III 433, BGE 144 III 481, BGE 130 V 343)
correctly but \emph{mischaracterises} the holding ---
omitting the load-bearing precedential element.  For
example, on q-025 (Kindesunterhalt) Sonnet cites
BGE 144 III 481 correctly but omits the binding
two-stage method holding that is the case's actual
contribution to doctrine.  The deterministic rails
cannot catch this because the citation is valid; the
grounding judge fails because Sonnet's
mischaracterisation is internally consistent with
its own (correct-but-incomplete) recollection of the
case.  We name this the \emph{real-citation /
misrepresented-holding} class and identify it as the
open problem for verification-first legal RAG.

\paragraph{Cost / latency.}
Across the 30 drafts, the four deterministic rails
together account for ${\sim}3$\,ms per draft (median
2.7\,ms; 95\,\% under 6\,ms), measured by wrapping
the rail dispatch in
\texttt{time.perf\_counter\_ns()} on the production
deployment.  The grounding rail takes 1.5--2.5\,s
wall-clock and ${\approx}\$0.005$ per draft via the
Anthropic API.  Total experimental cost (generation
$+$ audit $+$ judges): ${<\!}\$2$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When the deterministic rails miss.}
The case-citation rail catches \emph{fabricated}
references, not \emph{misused} ones.  For systems where
the underlying model has high citation-level fidelity
(as Sonnet 4.6 has on Swiss law: 3.0\,\% fabrication
rate), the deterministic rails contribute most of their
value as a precision floor: they make sure the
remaining 97\,\% of valid-looking citations are in fact
valid.  Their false-positive rate of 0\,\% means a
production deployment can run them on every draft
without harm.

\paragraph{When the judge rail miscalls.}
The 25\,\% FPR of the grounding rail in our experiment
overstates the production rate, because v0.2's
prior-only condition gives the judge little context.
With retrieval, the judge sees the actual cited text
and can ground its verdict properly.  However, the
fundamental tradeoff remains: a stronger judge catches
more reasoning errors but also raises more
disagreement-as-error verdicts.  Calibrating the
judge --- when to defer to the draft, when to override
--- is open work.

\paragraph{Generalisation.}
Each rail is corpus-agnostic.  The case-existence rail
needs an enumerable decision corpus; the statute rail
needs a statute mirror; the quote rail needs the cited
decisions' verbatim text.  None of these are
Swiss-specific.  An English-law adaptation
(e.g.\ England \& Wales) would require: (i) regex
patterns for English neutral citations and ICLR
references; (ii) a statute mirror for primary
legislation (\texttt{legislation.gov.uk}-derived);
(iii) decision full-text for the quote rail.  All
three are public-domain artefacts.  We expect the
catch-rate / FPR profile to be model-dependent: a
weaker generator with higher citation-level
fabrication rate will see higher rail TPR.

\paragraph{Privacy and re-identification.}
The corpus we publish (969\,k Swiss court decisions, CC0) consists
exclusively of decisions \emph{as published by the issuing court}
or via the official Swiss judicial publication portal
\texttt{entscheidsuche.ch}.  We perform no de-anonymisation, no
re-identification, and no enrichment with non-public data.
Nyffenegger, St{\"u}rmer \& Niklaus (2024) measured LLM-based
re-identification risk on anonymised Swiss court rulings and found
it minimal for top frontier models on \emph{court decisions}
specifically (substantially higher on Wikipedia entities); we
adopt their finding and discuss it explicitly in the dataset card.
Our contribution to privacy is one-direction: we expose the
already-public anonymised corpus through a more uniform interface,
which does not create re-identification capacity beyond what was
already feasible by direct scraping of the source portals.  We do
\emph{not} mirror the in-court (pre-anonymisation) corpus; that
work --- including the operational \emph{Anom2} system --- belongs
to the Niklaus, Mami{\'e} et al.\ (2023) line and the Federal
Supreme Court itself.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Benchmark size.}  v0.2 is a 30-question seed.  With
        only 6 wrong drafts, the per-configuration TPR estimates
        are imprecise: 95\,\% Wilson confidence intervals on a
        50\,\% point estimate ($n=6$) are $(15\%, 85\%)$.  We
        report exact ratios in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} so the
        reader can judge directly.  v1.0 (150 questions, named
        expert co-authors from Swiss law faculties; see
        \S\ref{sec:benchmark}) is the planned ``gold standard''
        deliverable.
  \item \textbf{Same model, generator and judge.}  Both roles are
        filled by Claude Sonnet 4.6 in our experiment.  This
        concentrates failure modes.  v1.0 will adopt a SwiLTra-Judge
        style independent calibrated judge (Niklaus et al., 2025),
        with human-expert validation, and report inter-judge
        agreement.
  \item \textbf{Prior-only condition.}  We do not report a
        retrieval-augmented ablation here because the paper's
        contribution is the audit pipeline, not the retrieval
        stack.  Production deployment at
        \texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch} runs the pipeline over a
        BM25\,/\,RRF\,/\,Haiku-rerank retriever; performance
        there is reported elsewhere.
  \item \textbf{Single-run experiment.}  The numbers in
        Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reflect one run; we do not
        bootstrap.  Run-to-run variance at \texttt{temperature=0}
        is small but non-zero.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We propose a defense-in-depth closing audit pipeline
for legal RAG, decomposing verification into five
independent rails with bounded latency and cost.  We
release Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2 as the first
multilingual benchmark for Swiss legal RAG.  Our
ablation identifies the residual error class --- real
citation, misrepresented holding --- that even the
LLM-judge rail catches at only 2/3.  Closing this gap
is the open problem.

The audit pipeline is live at
\url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch}, deployed on the entire
published Swiss federal and cantonal court corpus
(969,000+ decisions, daily refresh).  Code under MIT,
benchmark under CC0, corpus under CC0.  We invite
collaborators for v1.0.
