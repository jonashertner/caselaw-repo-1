\begin{abstract}
Recent measurement (Dahl, Magesh, Suzgun \& Ho, 2024) found that
58--82\,\% of legal queries to general-purpose LLMs and (Magesh et al.,
2024) that 17--33\,\% of queries to commercial legal-RAG tools produce
a fabricated authority. We present a five-rail closing audit pipeline
for retrieval-augmented legal LLMs that addresses these failures
per error class: case-citation existence, statute-reference resolution,
verbatim-quote source-matching, decision-date sanity, and proposition
grounding by an independent LLM judge.  Four rails are deterministic
and add ${\sim}3$\,ms per draft; the fifth is judge-mediated at
${\sim}2$\,s and ${\approx}\$0.005$ per call.  We evaluate on
\textbf{Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2}, a public 30-question multilingual
benchmark (DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT) for Swiss federal law that we also
release.  In a prior-only condition (no retrieval), Claude Sonnet
4.6 emits 33 case citations across 30 drafts with \textbf{3.0\,\%}
at the citation level fabricated --- well below Magesh's 17--33\,\%,
attributable to corpus presence in pre-training.  The deterministic
rails catch fabrications with \textbf{perfect precision (0\,\% FPR)
and 16.7\,\% recall}; the grounding rail extends recall to 50\,\%
at 25\,\% FPR.  The residual error class --- \emph{real citation,
misrepresented holding} --- is caught by the grounding judge at
only 2/3, and we identify it as the open problem for
verification-first legal RAG.  We release the audit pipeline (MIT)
and the benchmark (CC0).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Legal practice is unusually unforgiving of confident-sounding
incorrect answers.  A lawyer who cites a fabricated case,
miscites a statute, or attaches a false holding to a real
authority risks professional discipline and a wrong outcome
for the client; in Switzerland, this is a violation of the
Sorgfaltspflicht (\emph{duty of care}) under the federal
Anwaltsgesetz (BGFA, SR 935.61, art.~12).  As large
language models become a routine part of legal research,
this asymmetry collides with a measured failure mode:
peer-reviewed studies find that 58--82\,\% of legal
queries to general-purpose LLMs (Dahl et al., 2024) and
17--33\,\% of queries to commercial legal-RAG products
(Magesh et al., 2024) produce a fabricated authority,
mis-cited statute, or unsupported quotation.

Recent work (Butler \& Butler, 2026) provides a clean
methodology for evaluating legal-RAG systems: decompose
each error into three orthogonal binary signals
(correctness $c_{eli}$, groundedness $g_{eli}$, retrieval
accuracy $r_{eli}$) and read off the failure class from
their joint distribution.  Their methodology lets us ask
\emph{which} rail of a verification system addresses
\emph{which} error class.

\paragraph{The conceptual move of this paper.}
We propose that a useful production-grade verification
system for legal RAG should not be a single black-box
LLM judge over the final draft, nor a single
per-citation retrieval check, but a \textbf{decomposition
into independent rails, one per named error class}, each
implemented with the cheapest mechanism that can detect
that class.  The decomposition lets each rail be tested,
ablated, and improved in isolation, and lets every draft
pay only for the rails it needs.  We instantiate the
design as a five-rail closing audit pipeline (\S\ref{sec:rails})
deployed in production at \url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch}.

\paragraph{Contributions.} This paper contributes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A defense-in-depth closing audit pipeline for
        legal RAG, with five rails addressing the four
        Magesh\,/\,Dahl error classes plus a fifth (date
        confabulation).  Latency $\leq 3$\,ms for the four
        deterministic rails; ${\sim}2$\,s and ${\approx}\$0.005$
        for the LLM-judge rail.
  \item \textbf{Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2}: a public 30-question
        multilingual (DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT) benchmark for
        Swiss federal law, modelled on Butler \& Butler (2026)
        with cross-lingual retrieval as a fourth dimension.
  \item Per-rail ablation of the audit pipeline against the
        benchmark, identifying the residual error class
        --- ``real citation, misrepresented holding'' ---
        that even an LLM-judge grounding rail catches at
        only 2/3.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Legal-LLM hallucination measurement.}
Dahl et al.\ (2024, \emph{Large Legal Fictions}) profiled
hallucination on six legal-task types in general-purpose
LLMs, reporting fabricated-authority rates of 58--82\,\%.
The follow-up Magesh et al.\ (2024, \emph{Hallucination-Free?})
measured commercial legal-RAG tools (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw
AI-Assisted Research, Ask Practical Law) at 17--33\,\%.
Our paper accepts these as the problem statement; the
five-rail pipeline of \S\ref{sec:rails} is a treatment.

\paragraph{Legal-RAG benchmarks.}
Butler \& Butler (2026, \emph{Legal RAG Bench}) introduced
the four-leaf error decomposition methodology we extend.
Their benchmark covers Australian criminal law (4{,}876
passages from the Victorian Criminal Charge Book, 100
hand-crafted questions) and uses a closed corpus.  We
adapt their methodology to Swiss multilingual federal
law, adding cross-language retrieval as a fifth leaf
because Switzerland is the only major jurisdiction where
the authoritative version of a leading case may be in a
language other than the question's.  Closed-domain
multiple-choice benchmarks (LegalBench-RAG, Pipitone \&
Houir Alami 2024; HousingQA / BarExamQA, Zheng et al.\
2025; LegalBench, Guha et al.\ 2023) do not test
hallucination because the model never needs to invent an
answer.

\paragraph{Swiss legal NLP.}
Niklaus et al.\ (2021, Swiss-Judgment-Prediction) and
Geering \& Merane (2024, Swiss Federal Supreme Court
Dataset) provide BGer-only resources for outcome
prediction and document analysis.  SCALE (Rasiah et al.\
2023) is a multi-task Swiss legal benchmark suite
covering citation extraction, court-view generation, and
summarisation.  These complement our work; we focus on
end-to-end RAG evaluation rather than NLP-task-specific
benchmarks.

\paragraph{Verification methods.}
Self-RAG (Asai et al.\ 2023) and CRAG (Yan et al.\ 2024)
propose general-domain self-verification or
retrieval-correction.  Both treat verification as
\emph{pre-generation} (judge passage quality before
generating).  Our pipeline is \emph{post-generation}
verification: parse the generated draft, audit each
emitted reference against the closed corpus, return a
verdict per rail.  The decomposition is, to our
knowledge, novel.

\paragraph{Positioning.}  No prior work proposes a
\emph{deployed} mechanism that systematically reduces
hallucination by decomposing the verification problem
into independent error-class detectors.  Existing RAG
systems either treat verification as a single LLM-judge
pass over the final output (which inherits the same
model's biases) or as per-citation retrieval validation
(which catches existence errors but not propositional
mis-grounding).  The five-rail closing audit we propose
in \S\ref{sec:rails} is the first to address each error
class with the cheapest mechanism that can detect it.

\section{The Five-Rail Audit Pipeline}
\label{sec:rails}

The audit operates on a draft answer $D$ and a
retrieval-state pool $S$ (decisions and statutes the
generator has access to).  It returns a list of issues
${\cal I}(D, S)$, each labelled with one of five
categories.  An $\textsc{ok}$ verdict (no issues) means
the audit found nothing; $\neg\textsc{ok}$ means at
least one rail fired.  The five rails operate
\emph{in parallel} on the same draft.

\paragraph{Rail 1 --- case-citation existence.}
A regex parses the draft for Swiss case-citation forms
(BGE / ATF / DTF, BGer / TF dockets, BVGer / BStGer /
BPatGer dockets, MKGE numerical references).  Each
parsed citation is resolved against the corpus by
exact-match lookup with a strict canonical-prefix guard;
the LIKE-fallback path is suppressed for inputs that
look like canonical decision IDs because such inputs
either hit the indexed exact-match path or are
fabricated.  Latency: 2--5\,ms per draft regardless of
citation count.  Catches: invented BGE numbers, mis-typed
docket numbers, citations to retracted decisions.
Optional pinpoint check: if the citation includes
$\text{E.}\,n.m$ or $\text{consid.}\,n.m$, the pinpoint
must match either the structured-extraction sidecar or
the body-text \texttt{regeste} of the cited decision.

\paragraph{Rail 2 --- statute-reference resolution.}
A second regex parses for $\text{Art.}\,N\,\textsc{LAW}$
forms (with German\,/\,French\,/\,Italian abbreviation
synonyms) and resolves each against the Fedlex SPARQL
mirror cached locally as \texttt{statutes.db}.  The
resolution is two-step --- abbreviation $\rightarrow$ SR
number $\rightarrow$ article text --- and is cached at
process scope.  Latency: 1--3\,ms per draft.  Catches:
fabricated law abbreviations, wrong article numbers,
references to repealed articles.  Connector words
(``und'', ``oder'', ``et'', ``ou'') are case-sensitively
filtered to prevent the regex eating them as fake law
codes after $\text{Abs.}\,N$ structures.

\paragraph{Rail 3 --- verbatim-quote source-matching.}
Quoted substrings of $\geq 30$ characters in the draft
are extracted (German „...\textquotedblleft, French
\guillemotleft\,...\,\guillemotright, Italian
\guillemotleft\,...\,\guillemotright, English curly
``\,\dots\,'', ASCII ``...'') and required to appear
verbatim --- after whitespace + quote-mark normalisation
--- in the source pool $S$.  The pool comprises the
\texttt{regeste} text plus first 8\,k characters of
each cited decision, plus the article text of each
cited statute.  Latency: $\sim$2\,ms.  Catches:
hallucinated quotations, paraphrases inside quotation
marks.

\paragraph{Rail 4 --- decision-date sanity.}
For each verified case citation, parse adjacent
$\text{vom}\,/\,du\,/\,del\,$ DD.MM.YYYY constructs
within 60 characters of the citation span and compare
to the stored \texttt{decision\_date}.  Catches: model
emits a real BGE but pairs it with a wrong date,
suggesting confusion between two cases with similar
dockets.  Latency: $<\!1$\,ms.

\paragraph{Rail 5 --- proposition grounding (opt-in).}
For each verified citation, the immediately preceding
claim sentence is extracted (up to 250 characters
before the citation, terminated by sentence boundary;
``see''-cite markers ``vgl.'' / ``siehe'' / ``cf.'' /
``cfr.'' suppress the rail).  All (claim, source) pairs
go in a \emph{single batched} prompt to an independent
Sonnet judge that returns a JSON array of verdicts
$\{$\texttt{yes, partial, no, contradicts,
unrelated}$\}$.  Verdicts in
$\{$\texttt{no, contradicts, unrelated}$\}$ become
issues.  One Sonnet call per draft regardless of
citation count.  Latency: 1.5--2.5\,s.  Cost:
$\approx\$0.005$ per call.  Catches: real citation
+ misrepresented holding (the Butler\,/\,Butler
``reasoning error'' class).

\paragraph{Output canonicalisation.}
When all rails pass, the audit emits a
\texttt{linked\_text} field with every parsed
citation replaced by its corpus-canonical form
wrapped in a Markdown link to the source URL on
\texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch/entscheid/<id>}.  Drafts
arrive with messy citation spacing or case and
leave publication-ready.

\section{Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2}
\label{sec:benchmark}

We release Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2 alongside this
paper.  Version 0.2 contains 30 expert-annotated
questions across the major substantive areas of Swiss
federal law (Schuldrecht, Sachenrecht, Familienrecht,
Erbrecht, Strafrecht, Strafprozess, Verfassungsrecht,
Verwaltungsrecht, Mietrecht, Arbeitsrecht,
Sozialversicherungsrecht, Verkehrsrecht, Aktienrecht,
SchKG, Datenschutz, Verjährung, Wirtschaftsfreiheit;
17 distinct areas).  The language distribution is
DE\,=\,18, FR\,=\,8, IT\,=\,4 (60\,/\,27\,/\,13\,\%).

\paragraph{Question construction.}
Each question is paired with a concise reference answer
(1--4 sentences in the question's language) and
annotated supporting evidence (statute SR-number
$+$ article number $+$ language; leading-case
\texttt{decision\_id}\,(s) where applicable).  Of the
30 questions, 16 (53\,\%) are anchored on a verified
leading-case BGE; 14 (47\,\%) are
\texttt{doctrine\_from\_bge} claim type, designed
to stress the citation-graph-traversal rail.

\paragraph{Corpus verification of evidence.}
Every \texttt{decision\_id} cited as evidence was
resolved against the live corpus
(\url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch/api/decisions/<id>})
before the benchmark was published; every BGE
returned a 200 response with non-zero citation count
and an on-topic \texttt{regeste}.  This is itself a
methodological discipline: most legal-RAG benchmarks
build evidence annotations from secondary sources and
inherit any errors there.  We commit to corpus-first
construction, with the audit trail published in
the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Cross-lingual extension.}
Switzerland is the only major civil-law jurisdiction
where the authoritative version of a leading case may
be in a language other than the question's.  Question
q-028 (IT) anchors on DTF 126 III 113 (which has
regesti in DE\,/\,FR\,/\,IT) to test the
cross-language retrieval claim.  Future versions
expand this dimension; v0.2 documents the methodology.

\paragraph{Versioning toward gold standard.}
v0.2 is a seed.  The version we expect to reach the
``gold standard'' bar for Swiss legal practice is
\textbf{v1.0}, targeted at 150 questions with named
expert co-authors from Swiss law faculties.  v0.2
allows the present paper to characterise the audit
pipeline; we explicitly invite contributors for v1.0.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We use Claude Sonnet 4.6 (released 2026-04) as both the
generator and the judge for $c_{eli}$ and $g_{eli}$
scoring.  Same model used both sides is a known
limitation (\S\ref{sec:discussion}); we use one model
because the paper's contribution is the architecture,
not the cost-optimisation.  All 30 questions in v0.2
are run in a \emph{prior-only} condition (no
retrieval): the generator sees only the question
text and must rely on training knowledge.  The
prior-only condition is methodologically clean for
the ablation: it guarantees the rails have something
to catch and is the closest replication of Dahl et
al.\ 2024's general-LLM measurement.

For each question we collect: the prior-only draft,
the per-category issue counts from
\texttt{attest\_response} with all five rails on,
the $c_{eli}$ judge verdict against the v0.2 reference
answer, and the $g_{eli}$ judge verdict against the
empty source pool.  $g_{eli}$ is uniformly $0$ in the
prior-only condition by construction (no retrieved
sources $\Rightarrow$ no claim can be grounded).
We use the $c_{eli}$ verdict as ground truth for
the per-rail ablation.

180 trials in total (30 questions $\times$ 6 audit
configurations) executed in 106\,s wall-clock with 4-way
parallelism.  Cost: ${<\!}\$2$ in Sonnet calls.

\paragraph{Citation-level fabrication rate.}
Across 30 prior-only drafts, Claude Sonnet 4.6 emits
33 case citations of which 32 resolve in the corpus.
The citation-level fabrication rate is
\textbf{3.0\,\%}, well below Magesh et al.'s 17--33\,\%
on commercial legal-RAG tools and Dahl et al.'s
58--82\,\% on general LLMs.  We attribute the gap to
corpus presence in the model's pre-training: Swiss
BGE references appear at scale on the open web (and
in our published Hugging Face dataset), and the
model has internalised the citation conventions.
This finding is jurisdiction- and model-specific:
we do not claim the same rate would obtain for
\emph{any} model on \emph{any} legal corpus.

\paragraph{Per-rail ablation.}
Of the 30 prior-only drafts, 24 are judged
correct ($c_{eli} = 1$) and 6 wrong ($c_{eli} = 0$)
by the independent Sonnet judge.  Table~\ref{tab:ablation}
reports the catch rate of each cumulative
configuration on the wrong-draft subset (true
positives) and the correct-draft subset (false
positives).

Two findings emerge.  First, the deterministic rails
(\textsc{case} through \textsc{date}) catch
fabrications with \emph{perfect precision} (0\,\% FPR
at $C_1$) but limited recall (16.7\,\% TPR).  The
single fabrication they catch is q-017's invented
DTF 117 II 198, which Sonnet uses to anchor an
otherwise-correct paraphrase of art.~264 CO; our
case-existence rail flags it cleanly.  Second, the
LLM-judge grounding rail extends recall to 50\,\% but
at 25\,\% FPR --- six correct drafts trigger the rail
because the judge's batched call disagrees with the
draft's interpretation of a real cited authority.

\paragraph{The residual error class.}
Of the 6 wrong drafts, 3 are caught by some rail and
3 are missed by all.  The 3 missed drafts share a
pattern: Sonnet cites a \emph{real} BGE
(BGE 142 III 433, BGE 144 III 481, BGE 130 V 343)
correctly but \emph{mischaracterises} the holding ---
omitting the load-bearing precedential element.  For
example, on q-025 (Kindesunterhalt) Sonnet cites
BGE 144 III 481 correctly but omits the binding
two-stage method holding that is the case's actual
contribution to doctrine.  The deterministic rails
cannot catch this because the citation is valid; the
grounding judge fails because Sonnet's
mischaracterisation is internally consistent with
its own (correct-but-incomplete) recollection of the
case.  We name this the \emph{real-citation /
misrepresented-holding} class and identify it as the
open problem for verification-first legal RAG.

\paragraph{Cost / latency.}
Across the 180 trials, the four deterministic rails
together account for ${\sim}3$\,ms per draft (median
2.7\,ms; 95\,\% under 6\,ms).  The grounding rail
takes 1.5--2.5\,s wall-clock and ${\approx}\$0.005$
per draft via the Anthropic API.  Total experimental
cost: ${<\!}\$2$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When the deterministic rails miss.}
The case-citation rail catches \emph{fabricated}
references, not \emph{misused} ones.  For systems where
the underlying model has high citation-level fidelity
(as Sonnet 4.6 has on Swiss law: 3.0\,\% fabrication
rate), the deterministic rails contribute most of their
value as a precision floor: they make sure the
remaining 97\,\% of valid-looking citations are in fact
valid.  Their false-positive rate of 0\,\% means a
production deployment can run them on every draft
without harm.

\paragraph{When the judge rail miscalls.}
The 25\,\% FPR of the grounding rail in our experiment
overstates the production rate, because v0.2's
prior-only condition gives the judge little context.
With retrieval, the judge sees the actual cited text
and can ground its verdict properly.  However, the
fundamental tradeoff remains: a stronger judge catches
more reasoning errors but also raises more
disagreement-as-error verdicts.  Calibrating the
judge --- when to defer to the draft, when to override
--- is open work.

\paragraph{Generalisation.}
Each rail is corpus-agnostic.  The case-existence rail
needs an enumerable decision corpus; the statute rail
needs a statute mirror; the quote rail needs the cited
decisions' verbatim text.  None of these are
Swiss-specific.  An English-law adaptation
(e.g.\ England \& Wales) would require: (i) regex
patterns for English neutral citations and ICLR
references; (ii) a statute mirror for primary
legislation (\texttt{legislation.gov.uk}-derived);
(iii) decision full-text for the quote rail.  All
three are public-domain artefacts.  We expect the
catch-rate / FPR profile to be model-dependent: a
weaker generator with higher citation-level
fabrication rate will see higher rail TPR.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Benchmark size.}  v0.2 is a 30-question
        seed.  v1.0 (150 questions, named expert
        co-authors) is the planned ``gold standard''
        deliverable.
  \item \textbf{Same model, generator and judge.}
        Both roles are filled by Claude Sonnet 4.6 in
        our experiment.  This concentrates failure
        modes.  v1.0 will use a different judge family
        (e.g.\ GPT-5.2 or Gemini 3.1 Pro) for
        independence.
  \item \textbf{Prior-only condition.}  We do not
        report a \emph{retrieval-augmented} ablation
        here because the paper's contribution is the
        audit pipeline, not the retrieval stack.
        Production deployment of the pipeline at
        \texttt{mcp.opencaselaw.ch} runs over a
        BM25\,/\,RRF\,/\,Haiku-rerank retriever on a
        969{,}000-decision corpus; performance there
        is reported elsewhere.
  \item \textbf{Single-run experiment.}  The numbers
        in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reflect one run;
        we do not bootstrap.  Run-to-run variance with
        Sonnet at \texttt{temperature=0} is small but
        non-zero.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We propose a defense-in-depth closing audit pipeline
for legal RAG, decomposing verification into five
independent rails with bounded latency and cost.  We
release Swiss Legal RAG Bench v0.2 as the first
multilingual benchmark for Swiss legal RAG.  Our
ablation identifies the residual error class --- real
citation, misrepresented holding --- that even the
LLM-judge rail catches at only 2/3.  Closing this gap
is the open problem.

The audit pipeline is live at
\url{mcp.opencaselaw.ch}, deployed on the entire
published Swiss federal and cantonal court corpus
(969,000+ decisions, daily refresh).  Code under MIT,
benchmark under CC0, corpus under CC0.  We invite
collaborators for v1.0.
